Orientation to the Drift

Commonweal civic guidance for citizens of the Syndicated Technocratic Republic beyond the frontier. Unit purchased secondhand at Free Harbor Tamsin.

Known space is a long lane of stars, about a hundred parsecs end to end. The Hallowed Dominion of Corinthia holds the west, gathered around the black hole it worships as the Eye of God, and its prayers are answered. The Syndicated Technocratic Republic (STR) holds the east: dozens of systems, each owned by a different corporation, held together by contract and by Commonweal. Between them, in a belt some 26 parsecs wide, lies the Drift, independent systems that the Treaty of the Lantern keeps out of either power's hands.

At the Drift's heart is the Lantern, a precursor structure both powers claimed in the same decade and nearly destroyed each other over. Fortress pickets watch it from both borders: Vigil's Reach on the Corinthian side, Sable Station on the STR's. North, the Null Corridor runs border to border, a dead zone that is also the shortest route between the powers. South, the Grave of Ash marks where the last war ended.

There has been no open war since CY 274. The powers now compete through trade contracts, missionaries, and bribes.

Commonweal notice

Free Harbor Tamsin, just south of the Lantern, is the Drift's open port. Charter law does not apply there, though Commonweal keeps every visiting citizen's tier on record.

DOC CW-HIS-0212CLEARANCE: PUBLICCHARTER YEARS (CY)

Public record

Corinthian dates run about 730 years ahead of the Syndicated Technocratic Republic's.

  1. −730
    Out of the dark. Corinthian scripture says the faithful followed the Eye's call to the Throne.
  2. CY 0
    Charter of Meridian. Settler companies sign a compact to honor each other's contracts. The Syndicated Technocratic Republic dates its calendar from the signing.
  3. CY 31
    Commonweal incorporated. Hired to keep the Charter's records. Within a century it also runs customs.
  4. CY 58
    The Combine. Three charter companies merge into the Halvorsen Combine.
  5. CY 140
    First contact. Surveyors and pilgrims meet in the Drift, and both lay claim to the Lantern.
  6. 141–190
    The Border Wars. Vigil's Reach and Halcyon Exchange are built to win them.
  7. CY 196
    Tamsin hollowed. Smugglers carve a port into an asteroid.
  8. CY 233
    The Arbitration Clause. Every charter company accepts Commonweal's courts as binding. Holdouts lose their trade licenses within the year.
  9. CY 251
    Copperline rush. Money from the Syndicated Technocratic Republic floods the Drift.
  10. 272–274
    The Ash War. The two battle fleets destroy each other at the Grave of Ash.
  11. CY 273
    The Ninth Pontifex dies. His funeral barge vanishes. The Tenth is crowned.
  12. CY 274
    Treaty of the Lantern. No annexing the Drift. No landing on the Lantern. Commonweal signs for every charter company.
  13. CY 281
    Council of Captains. Tamsin's captains begin settling the Drift's disputes.
  14. CY 298
    Free Labor League. Founded at Varga. Banned by every charter company the same year.
  15. 310–311
    Lean years. Psalter's harvests fail. Bread prices double across Corinthia.

How the charter system works

  • Each system is owned outright by a charter company. Its laws are the company handbook; its police are company security.
  • Companies trade, sue, poach, and undercut each other constantly. Open fighting between charters is rare, because Commonweal fines it into ruin.
  • Commonweal owns no worlds but Meridian. It keeps the citizenship ledger, sets your tier, runs the courts, and clears every transaction. A company it blacklists cannot do business.
  • No one outside knows who owns Commonweal. Its shares have never traded.

Potential Origins

Commonweal profiles of the people most often found in the Drift, and where they came from.

◆ Corinthia◆ Syndicated Technocratic Republic◆ The Drift◆ Hazard

The faithful

Clergy, pilgrims, and lapsed believers. Courtesy and distance are advised.

  • Choral Order singer who lost the faith
  • Open Eye novice with too many questions
  • Old Rite hedge-priest from Saint Vey
  • Deserter from the Wandering Choir
Enforcers

Persons trained to violence by an institution, now without one.

  • Inquisitor who walked away
  • Sable Command deserter
  • Company security, contract terminated
Debtors and runaways

Persons with unresolved obligations. Most will lie about them.

  • Halvorsen debtor who skipped out
  • Psalter farmhand fleeing famine
  • Disowned heir to a charter family
  • Vespers monk who broke the silence
Spacers

Pilots and crews who live between ports.

  • Marrow skimmer pilot
  • Null Corridor smuggler
  • Longreach scout, last of the crew
  • Halcyon freight hauler with a bad manifest
Salvagers and diggers

Persons who take things out of the ground or out of wrecks.

  • Ashborn salvager from the Grave
  • Old Rite miner from Saint Vey
  • Reliquary digger with something in a pocket
Agitators and insiders

Persons who know how institutions work, and resent it.

  • Free Labor League organizer on the run
  • Commonweal clerk who read the wrong file
  • Halcyon broker with a grudge
  • Lay Brother who spoke at the bread riots
Scholars and engineers

Persons whose training is valuable to every party. Expect recruitment offers.

  • Assay Institute researcher
  • Open Eye scholar-monk
  • Ostrava engineer
  • Former Treaty Watch observer
The touched

Persons showing phenomena no one can explain. Commonweal requests all sightings be reported.

  • Born too close to the Eye
  • Raised in sight of the Lantern
  • Hears a voice in the static
  • Survived a Null Corridor crossing that should have killed them
