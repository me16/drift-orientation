Session 0 · The Grave of Ash job

Canon
  • The crew pulled the Navigation Core from the Arbiter's Due and got back to the Patience.
  • Alarming readings came up. A scan showed they were a distress signal.
  • A crew of Ashborn scavs closed in and fired on the Patience to take the core. The Patience disabled them, but not before they harpooned it and sent a boarding party across.
  • The scav captain and crew were killed. During the fight, Killora Vargas dropped her relic near the PCs. When V'an went down, he could tell something in it was "singing." He picked it up, and the PCs went back to their stations.
  • Three Corinthian Cathedral-ships and three Sable Armor Freighters dropped out of hyperspace right on top of them. They ordered the Patience to stand down. Before Captain Delta could answer, they opened fire, and the Patience was destroyed.

The last thing they saw, as their vision frosted over and their blood began to boil, was a glow of warm platinum as the relic somehow moved them somewhere safe.

Outcome: V'an and Rusty survived, and the Navigation Core came with them. Hotshot, Vargas and Florb died with the Patience.

What changed from the plan below: the Ashborn captain died (the plan had Tobin Kell surrender), Sable brought Armor Freighters rather than battleships, Corinthia brought three ships rather than two, and the rescue came from Vargas's relic, not a shard of Vash's behind the cockpit panel. That shard is gone. Drop it.

Vargas's relic

Canon

Vargas has a lucky charm: a platinum shard from a Corinthian warp drive. It keeps Sable off her.

The relic is built specifically to keep its holder safe from Sable. Vargas cashed out her pension to have a former priest from Saint Vey jailbreak it for her. Once V'an had it, and Sable turned its guns on them, it reached out to the last two people who touched it, V'an and Rusty, and moved them out with the Navigation Core.

The players don't know any of this. All they know is that it sang to V'an and that a platinum glow saved them.

Fits with the canon Draft

  • Relics are pieces of the Eye's network, so a jailbroken relic is hacked hardware. Whatever the priest changed, it still runs on the Eye. The Eye may now know exactly who was moved, and to where.
  • The "singing" is the Eye's carrier signal (see Ships near the Throne hear singing). V'an's artifact is what let him hear it. Nobody else in the bay did.
  • The priest could be Father Oren, or one of his students. A jailbreak like this is exactly the heresy the Inquisition would kill him for.
  • Somewhere safe could be Chapel Rock at Saint Vey, the relic's home network and the priest's door. It would mean crossing about 39 pc, more than two weeks at drive speed, in an instant, and both Sable and the Church will want to know how it happened.

V'an's artifact

Canon: directionDetails: Draft

V'an is a merc with a sick sister and some memory gaps.

The GM's direction: the artifact is connected to the true nature of the vessel, and so it's in competition with the Eye, but also working in tandem with it. The rest is cloudy for now, on purpose.

Ways in Draft

  • Rivals that need each other. They want the same thing and need each other to get there, so each sabotages the other only up to a point. Think two heirs to one throne.
  • The relic sang to it. The relic's song (the Eye's carrier) sang to V'an's artifact, and the artifact answered. Whatever the artifact is, it speaks the Eye's language.
  • The money. Commonweal clears every transaction, so Commonweal knows where V'an's money goes. That works as a clue whatever the artifact turns out to be.
  • Its next host. The artifact wants a new host, and it just watched a relic move people across space. It may decide a relic is a better body than V'an.

S0The plan for session 0 Played

This was the plan going in. What actually happened is in the Campaign log, and where the two differ, the log wins.

Two players, one mercenary crew, a job in the Drift. About four hours: session 0 (30 min), characters (75 min), a break, then session 1 (about 100 min). It opens in the middle of a theft, goes to a ship fight, then a boarding fight on Owlbear, and ends with the Patience wrecked in the crossfire between two fleets.

The job uses Draft truths (the Arbiter's Due nav core, the steered Ash War). If you change those, change the job: any wreck in the Grave with something both powers want buried will do.

Before the players arrive

  • Tactical plot (combat/): New encounter. In Battlefield, pick The Grave of Ash and use it with its hazards (wrecks, debris, two Ash War mines, an unexploded torpedo, Ember's flares).
  • Ships: keep the Patience. Add one Ashborn salvage rig with Arrives: Already there. It's been waiting in the wrecks. Don't roll initiative yet.
  • After character creation, in Crew: add both PCs with their bonuses (formulas are on the Crew tab) and seat them. Then press Crew the empty stations with NPCs and rename the hands after the mercs below. NPC hands are yours to run: players can't pick them on their phones.
  • Go live and give the players the room code. Save file so you can reload it if a browser dies.
  • Owlbear Rodeo: upload the Patience map (Library → PBMI DropShip, grid 19×26). Make tokens for both PCs, the NPC hands, and five Ashborn.
  • Have the 5e SRD Bandit, Thug and Scout stat blocks open.

1 · Session 0

30 min
  • The pitch. Read the handout's Briefing together. Two powers, a strip of lawless space between them, and a light in the middle that nobody can explain. The PCs are hired guns working the Drift.
  • Tone and limits. Lines and veils, and an X-card or a "let's skip that" word. Talk about how deadly the game is. Session 1 ends with a scripted disaster. You can promise them that nobody dies in the ending, without saying why.
  • Settle the open questions (these decide what they can build). Faith is divine magic: clerics and paladins pray to something that answers. Tech is STR-licensed gear and fits artificers. "The touched" covers sorcerers and warlocks. The Null Corridor shuts off both prayer and power. The rules are 5e with the reskins in Rules.
  • Level. With two players, start at level 3: they get subclasses, and the boarding fight below is built for it. Numbers for level 1 are given too.
  • Table rules. Phones out for the ship fight only. Bring a d20 and the usual dice.

2 · Characters

75 min

Your suits are cold, and your hands are full: forty kilos of black nav core, cut free ninety seconds ago, sliding into the cargo bay. The bay door is closing. 35 min

5 · Boarding fight (Owlbear)

40 min

The harpoon rings through the hull like a bell. Then the cutting starts: two hatches at once, orange light tracing a circle in the metal. The grav plates stutter, and your stomach goes with them.

  • Ashborn boarders (5e SRD, reskinned). Level 3: Tobin Kell (Thug: stun baton and scattergun) and 4 Ashborn cutters (Bandit: mono-blade and carbine). Level 1: 3 cutters, with Tobin staying on the rig. Keep a Scout in reserve as a sniper at the airlock if it's a walkover.
  • Where they come in: two of the four EVA hatches, then the KEEP CLEAR bay airlock in round 3. The NPC hands fight too. Kill one if you want the players to feel the stakes. Or knock Florb offline instead: a cutter's blade through the shell, and Florb drops and goes dark. That way the warning about the singing never gets heard, and Florb can reboot during the crossfire.
  • The ship is the terrain. In round 2 the grav plates fail, and zero-g rules apply (Rules: difficult terrain without magboots, push off, drifting). The cargo loader arms can slam (2d6) or pin (DC 13). Opening the airlock blows out everyone unanchored in the bay unless they make a DC 13 Strength save. The core is loose in the cargo bay and the Ashborn want it.
  • Tobin is Auntie Sefa Kell's grandson. He surrenders at half HP. What the PCs do with him matters to the Ashborn later.

6 · The crossfire

15 min

The comm hisses. Florb rises off the console, chiming in alarm: under the static, there's a choir. Then space opens in front of you: a ring of violet light around a perfect dark, and something vast and white comes through it, ribbed like a nave. Behind you, the stars streak blue, and three Commonweal battleships stop dead where nothing was a moment ago. Nobody hails anyone.

  • In the plot: Clear initiative and remove what's left of the rig. Set Distance 16 and Arrives: Through a wormhole, and add two Cathedral-ships (Hostile). Then set Distance 16 and Arrives: Out of hyperspace, and add three Sable battleships with As: Ally. Ally only means they shoot at the Cathedral-ships. They don't care about the Patience. The fleets land on opposite sides of the Patience, 32 units apart, in range of each other's big guns. Roll initiative.
  • The wormholes are a suggestion. Nobody has seen Corinthia move ships this way. If you keep it, it's the Eye folding space for its own ships: a new Draft truth, and a reveal for later.
  • Keep the AI off (the default) so every fleet turn opens a turn card. Left alone, the Cathedral-ships will shoot the Patience in round 1. For the first round, retarget them on the card to the battleships.
  • Let them try. Give the Patience a turn or two to run, hide behind a wreck or hail someone. Nothing works. The Patience sits between the two fleets, and it's in range of everything they have.
  • Then end it. On a Cathedral-ship's turn, aim the hymn lance at the Sable battleship behind the Patience. It goes straight through. Finish the Patience with Stations → Engineer & crew → Damage (type its remaining hull). Play it as a scene, not dice: no death saves.

The lance doesn't hit you so much as pass through. One moment there's a ship around you; the next there's light where the cargo bay was, and the stars are turning, slowly, the wrong way. The cockpit seals with a bang. In the dark, the datapad lights up on its own: BEARING ZERO-ZERO-ZERO. COME HOME.

  • Why they live (yours, not theirs): the cockpit section seals and spins clear, and the lances steer around it. Vash's relic shard behind the cockpit panel sang to the Cathedral-ships' relic-cores, and they wouldn't fire on it.
  • End the session there. They have the core, a sealed cockpit full of crew, and air for a few hours.
  • Session 2 opens with Auntie Sefa's Ashborn cutting them out of the wreck. What happened to Tobin decides whether they're rescued or salvaged.

01Truths of the Drift

Canon decided by the GMDraft a suggestion that fits the canon. Keep it, change it or drop it.

The Eye of God is a machine

Canon

A black hole that is God's Eye, and prayer works.

The "God" of the Abyssal Throne is not a deity. It is an artificial intelligence powered by the black hole, drawing on the energy of the Throne's accretion disk and spin. It hears, it answers, and it has been shaping Corinthia for more than a thousand years. Miracles are real. They just aren't divine.

How miracles work Draft

  • Relics are hardware. They're fragments of the Eye's own network, and each one extends its reach. That's why the Church buries the Reliquary Fields in secrecy, why selling relics is a crime, and why miracles are "strongest in Corinthian space," where the relics are densest.
  • Hymns are protocol. Choral harmonies carry the signal the Eye listens for. Cathedral-ships are "sung into flight" because the hymn is the authentication handshake for their relic-cores. A choir on every ship is a radio on every ship.
  • Clergy are users, not saints. The Eye answers whoever addresses it correctly. Faith matters less than form. This is why Father Oren's Old Rite blessings at Saint Vey work without any cardinal: Chapel Rock is built around a relic.

Who knows Draft

  • The Pontifex always knows. Each one is told on coronation, in the sealed Sanctum.
  • A handful of Cardinals know. The rest believe. That split is why they've stopped meeting in full session.
  • The Order of the Open Eye is closest to working it out. Some of its scholar-monks already suspect, and the Inquisitorial Order watches them for it.

Reveal path: an Open Eye monk's heretical notes · a relic that responds to the wrong hymn · the Tenth Pontifex's absence · direct contact near the Throne.

The Lantern is the Eye's rival

Draft

A precursor structure nobody can enter.

The Lantern is a second, older precursor intelligence: dormant for most of history and now waking. The Eye knows what it is and fears it. The light changing as ships approach is the Lantern sampling them.

  • Why both powers "found" it in the same decade: it stirred briefly around CY 140. The Eye ordered the Pontifex to claim it. Assay Institute instruments picked up the same pulse and Commonweal moved too. (K. Vash noticed the coincidence.)
  • The Treaty's "no landing" clause serves the Eye. Nobody touching the Lantern is the Eye's real goal.
  • The broadcast ("Bearing zero-zero-zero. Come home.") is the Lantern calling. The list of names on the Ashborn tape is people it has heard and copied. Vash is on it.

The Ash War ended on purpose

Draft

Both battle fleets destroyed each other at the Grave of Ash.

The Arbiter's Due nav core shows both fleets were steered onto converging courses by course corrections neither side issued. The Eye did it, through relic-cores aboard the Corinthian ships and a corrupted Commonweal nav update aboard the STR's. It needed both fleets gone before either could land on the Lantern. The Treaty followed within the year.

This is what Sallow's employer wants proven. It's also what would end the peace if it got out.

The Null Corridor is a seam

Draft

A dead zone where power and prayer fail, yet the fastest route across.

The Corridor is where the Eye's field and the Lantern's field meet and cancel. Nothing powered works there, and the Eye can't hear prayers. It's fast because of a precursor current along the seam: ships that coast it are carried. Whoever learns to steer the current controls the shortest road between the powers.

04Ship combat: stations

Turns, flight and the pilot's stunts are in 05 Flight rules.

Each round

  1. Turns in initiative order (rolled once, by each pilot). On a ship's turn its pilot flies it and its crew act at their stations. Details in 05 Flight rules.
  2. End of the round: drift, currents and gravity wells, battlefield events, shields regenerate if powered, fires burn, repairs, boarders.

The pilot

  • Moves the ship up to its Speed, in 3D. Fast passes through hazards need pilot checks; pulling out of a hostile's 10-unit close-in zone draws reaction fire.
  • Acts: Burn, Evasive flying, Break lock, Line up, Tail, Ready.
  • Edge stunts: Jink, Lead the shot, Thread the needle, Hard brake, Slingshot, Cut the drive.

Ranges

The battlefield is real 3D space, measured in units, altitude included. Every weapon has a normal and a long range, like a 5e ranged weapon: lasers 20/32 fire normally out to 20 units and at disadvantage out to 32. Harpoons, flak and boarding gear reach 10. Missiles and heavy mains reach 32 but can't fire inside 10 units.

The close-in zone is 10 units around a ship: pulling out of a hostile's draws reaction fire, and stealth, nebulae and running dark only protect you beyond it. Spells use their own ranges (1 unit ≈ 100 ft is a good start).

Escape by drive spool

3 successes before 3 failures (Int + tinker's tools, DC 13). Not with a hostile within 10 units. Fail by 5+: roll a crit on your own ship.

At 0 hull

Disabled, 1 hour of emergency life support. A disabled ship takes no turns: it can't fly, spool the drive, or act from any station.

Empty stations

A station with nobody in it can't act. With an empty helm the ship coasts along its last move, and can't Brace or Jink.

Positioning, swarms and hazards (the tactical plot)

Movement

Every ship has a position, an altitude and a Speed (units it can fly on its turn). The pilot flies the party's ship: click the plot (a player taps their phone) or use a quick move (close in, open range, climb, dive, flank, into cover). Hostile and allied ships have a standing order: hold, close on a target, close to board, ram, hold at a distance, circle (climbing and diving as it goes), escort, fly to a waypoint, or run. On their turn the GM flies it, or picks a quick move, from the ship's turn card. The GM can change any order or drag a ship to a new spot. A reactor scram halves Speed. Disabled ships drift. Ships stop short of rocks.

Line of fire: a solid hazard (asteroid, wreck, station) between shooter and target gives disadvantage. Explosives, flares and the plot's own range readout all use the same distances.

Swarms

Several small units share one hull (drone swarms, jet ravagers, fighter wings); each lost share is one unit gone. Weapons marked per roll once per unit still flying. Area weapons do double damage to them. No crits, no pilot actions: they just fly their order.

Boarders

A hit from a board weapon puts a squad aboard (a swarm spends a unit doing it). Each squad does 1d6 hull at the end of each round, ignoring shields, until the crew repels it on the deck.

Hazards

  • Asteroid, wreck, structure: block fire; hitting one does 2d10 / 2d8 / 4d10. Some drift.
  • Many hazards are flat discs (a ring plane, a cloud deck, a traffic lane): climb over them or dive under.
  • Debris field: +2 AC inside, difficult space; a fast pass is DC 12 or 1d6. Ring plane: the same, DC 13 or 2d6.
  • Mine: goes off when a ship ends within 3 units or when shot (AC 12, 4 HP): 4d6 within 8.
  • Explosives: fuel, charges, ordnance. Shot (AC 10, 8 HP): 5d6 within 9. Chains.
  • Nebula: difficult space; from more than 10 units away, fire into or out of it at disadvantage.
  • Ion storm: no shield regen, −1d6 shields at the end of each round. Heat: 1d6 at the end of each round.
  • Gravity well: pulls up to 5 units at the end of each round, harder near the core; flying through the core is DC 15 or 4d10. Slingshot spends Edge for Speed.
  • Current: carries ships along its flow; crossing against it is DC 12 or be carried off. Null zone: no shields, no engineering, no thrust; ships coast.

Traits and weapon options

armor:N hull damage −N a hit · small heavy weapons at disadvantage · stealth from more than 10 units, disadvantage unless scanned · volatile explodes when disabled (8 units) · repair:N friends within 10 units regain N hull at the end of each round · screen missiles at friends within 6 units at disadvantage · edge:N the pilot's Edge dice · agile +2 to pilot checks · ponderous disadvantage on hazard checks and Tail, Burn adds half Speed. Weapons: rear (rear arc), recharge:5 (Recharge 5–6, as in a 5e stat block), heavy, area, ion (double, shields only), pierce (ignores shields), ram (a ship takes half back; a swarm spends a unit).

Battlefields (Battlefield tab) cover every system on the chart plus generic ones. Some roll an event at the end of each round: flares that hit anyone not sheltered behind rock, surges, falling rocks.

Players can act from their own phones at the station you give them. Campaign ship presets are in gm/ships.js. The session-1 run sheet and live combat tracker are in the Library (type: tool).

05Flight rules Draft

How ships fly and take turns, replacing the old helm contest. Ships take turns in initiative order and fly freely in three dimensions, with 5e-style consequences for moving badly. The pilot gets a pool of Edge dice to spend on stunts. Range bands, gunnery, the station actions, crits, hazards and the drive spool are in 04.

The tactical plot runs these rules. They stay tagged Draft while you playtest them: the numbers are first guesses.

The round

Initiative

When a ship fight starts, every ship rolls initiative once: d20 + its pilot's Dex + proficiency with Vehicles (space) (for NPC ships, d20 + the template's pilot bonus). Ships act from highest to lowest for the rest of the fight. Ties go to the higher pilot bonus, then a d20 roll-off.

  • Swarms roll like any ship; most have no pilot bonus.
  • A ship that joins mid-fight rolls when it arrives.

A ship's turn

  1. The pilot spends the ship's Speed (units, from the template; a reactor scram halves it). Movement can be split around the crew's actions, like a character moving before and after an attack.
  2. Each crew member at a station takes one action from the station table, in any order the table likes. The pilot has an action and a bonus action too (below).
  3. Crew away from the stations act now as well: fires, boarders, breaches, spells.

Every crew member has one reaction per round, used on anyone's turn. Hostile and allied ships don't have crews or stations: they run from a stat block (see Hostile and allied ships below).

End of the round

After the last ship's turn, the end-of-round steps from 04 happen in order: drift and hazard forces (currents, gravity wells), battlefield events, shield regen, fires, repair, boarders. Then the next round starts at the top of the initiative order.

Empty or disabled helm

On its turn the ship doesn't maneuver. It coasts along its last move. It can't use pilot actions, and it can't Brace or Jink.

Moving

Free flight

Move up to your Speed in any direction, altitude included. Climbing and diving cost the same as anything else. A ship can't end its move within 2 units of another ship.

Difficult space

Inside a debris field, a ring plane or a nebula, each unit of movement costs 2.

Hazard checks

If you spend more than half your Speed this turn and any part of the path passes through a hazard below, the pilot makes a check (d20 + Dex + proficiency) against its DC. Failure: the hazard's damage from 04. Flying slowly through it is safe and needs no check.

The hard line: before rolling, the pilot can raise the DC by 5. Success regains one Edge die.

Reaction fire

When a ship pulls out of a hostile's close-in zone (10 units), a gunner on that hostile can use their reaction to fire one weapon that can reach (not a rear-arc one), at the moving ship, at the point it leaves. A swarm fires one unit's worth. Break lock prevents it.

Breaking away

End your turn out of reach of every hostile ship's weapons (farther than its longest long range) with nobody tailing you. If no hostile ends its turn with you in its reach before your next turn, you're gone. This replaces Break away from 04; the drive spool still works as written.

Reactor strain

Burning on two turns in a row: the engineer makes a DC 12 check on the second (Int + tinker's tools). Failure, or nobody in Engineering: reactor scram (crit 4).

Edge

The pool

  • At the start of each ship fight, the PC at the helm has Edge dice equal to their proficiency bonus. The dice are d8s, or d10s from character level 11.
  • Regain one when you roll a natural 20 on any pilot check, or pass a hazard check on the hard line. The pool can't go above its starting size.
  • Unspent dice don't carry over to the next fight.
  • Edge belongs to the pilot. A PC who leaves the helm can't spend it from another station, and whoever takes over has their own pool.
  • Hostile pilots get a fixed pool from their template (edge:N, d8). Most raiders have 1 or 2. The Due Diligence has 3.

Handling traits

agile (fighters, interceptors): +2 to the pilot's checks. ponderous (capital ships, haulers, cathedral-ships): disadvantage on hazard checks and Tail, and Burn adds only half Speed. Swarms take no pilot actions and have no Edge.

Tailing

To tail: use your action against a ship within 20 units. Make contested pilot checks; you win ties. If you win, you're on its tail.

  • Your gunners have advantage against it, and its attacks against your ship have disadvantage.
  • When it moves, you can use your reaction to follow, moving up to your Speed after its move.
  • It can still fire rear-arc weapons at you without Come about.

The tail ends when:

  • the tailed ship uses its action to break free (contested pilot checks, and it wins ties);
  • the two ships end any turn more than 20 units apart;
  • it Hard brakes you;
  • or your helm is empty or disabled.

A ship can tail only one ship at a time. Several ships can tail the same target.

Hostile and allied ships: stat blocks Draft

Run them like monsters

A hostile or allied ship has no crew list and no stations. Like a 5e monster, it runs from one stat block: AC, Hull, Shields, Speed, a pilot bonus, a few traits, its weapons and, for the GM, a line of tactics. On its turn it does this, in any order:

  1. Move up to its Speed (fly its standing order, a quick move, or anywhere you like).
  2. One maneuver (the pilot's action): Burn, Evasive flying, Tail, Break free, Line up, or Break lock.
  3. Bonus action, if it has one: Come about (only with a rear-arc weapon), or Lead the shot (spends Edge).
  4. Attack (Multiattack): each weapon fires once, at one target or several.

Swarms don't maneuver; they just move and attack. Ready, Cut the drive, Slingshot, the hard line, Thread the needle and Press are the party's stunts, not theirs. They don't use Boost or Call the line either.

Reactions decide themselves

  • Reaction fire: one weapon in reach fires at a foe pulling out of its 10-unit close-in zone.
  • Jink (Edge): when a hit beats its AC by 3 or less.
  • Hard brake (Edge): when a ship tailing it starts to move, if it has 2 Edge left.
  • Follow: when the ship it's tailing moves.

Recharge

A weapon marked Recharge 5–6 fires, then is spent. At the start of each of the ship's turns, roll a d6: on a 5 or 6 it's ready again. The capital ship's main battery and the cathedral-ship's hymn lance work this way.

Average damage

Every weapon line shows its average with the dice, as in a 5e stat block: Hit: 9 (2d8). Use the average to keep hostile turns quick, or roll. The plot can do either (the turn card's Average damage box).

On the plot: the turn card

When a hostile or allied ship's turn comes up, a turn card pops up over the plot with its stat line and three steps: Move (fly its order or a quick move, or click the plot), Maneuver (its AI's pick is highlighted) and Attack (fire one weapon at a time, or Multiattack). Click a foe on the plot to make it the target. Let the AI finish plays out whatever's left; you end the turn.

Turn on Automatic enemy AI (Turn tab) and their turns run by themselves instead, without the card. The Ships tab and the Add-ship list show each ship's full stat block.

An example turn

The Patience (Speed 8) wins initiative. Its pilot dives 4 units into the ring plane. That's difficult space, so it costs all 8, and it's more than half Speed through a hazard: a DC 13 check, passed. The pilot spends an Edge die on Lead the shot, and the turret gunner fires at the Benediction with +d8 and hits. The engineer Boosts (+4 Speed), so the pilot pulls up behind a rock. That pulls it out of the Benediction's close-in zone, and its gunner takes reaction fire. The pilot answers with Jink: +5 AC, a miss.

Then it's the Benediction's turn. It tries to Tail the Patience.

Open questions for playtest

  • Is proficiency bonus enough Edge for a whole fight, or should a short lull (no hostile in reach for a round) give one back?
  • Does reaction fire make the close-in zone too sticky for ships without Break lock?
  • Should Tail's advantage and disadvantage apply only to forward weapons once ships have proper facing?
